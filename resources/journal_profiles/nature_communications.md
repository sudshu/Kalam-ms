# Nature Communications

## Overview
- **Full name**: Nature Communications
- **Publisher**: Springer Nature
- **Scope**: High-quality research in all areas of biological, physical, chemical, and Earth sciences. Significant advances that represent a substantial step forward.
- **Audience**: Broad scientific audience
- **Impact Factor**: ~17 (2024)
- **Open Access**: Yes (Gold OA, CC-BY)

## Article Types
| Type | Word Limit | Figures | Abstract |
|------|-----------|---------|----------|
| Article | No strict limit (~5,000 words typical) | 10 display items | 150 words, unstructured |
| Review | ~6,000 words | 10 display items | 150 words |
| Perspective | ~3,000 words | 4 display items | 150 words |

## Formatting
- **Sections**: Abstract, Introduction, Results, Discussion, Methods. No numbered sections.
- **References**: Numbered in order of appearance, superscript. Typically 50-70.
- **Citation style**: Numbered, superscript (same as Nature family)
- **Data Availability**: REQUIRED. Prominent statement after Methods.
- **Code Availability**: REQUIRED if computational work involved.
- **Author Contributions**: REQUIRED (CRediT format encouraged).
- **Competing Interests**: REQUIRED.

## LaTeX
- **Document class**: No official class. Use `article` or Nature template.
- **Bibliography style**: `naturemag.bst`
- **Template**: Springer Nature provides a generic template.

## Writing Style
- More detailed than Nature — can include more methodology in main text
- Still accessible to a broad audience
- Results and Discussion are separate sections (not combined)
- Methods go at the end (after Discussion) — can be detailed
- Supplementary Information for additional analyses, figures, tables
- Active voice, clear quantitative statements

## What Reviewers Look For
- Significant advance in understanding
- Rigorous and transparent methodology
- Reproducibility (data/code availability)
- Clear presentation of results
- Balanced Discussion (implications AND limitations)

## Tips for Acceptance
- Open access = broader readership = more citations (if the paper is good)
- Lower bar than Nature but still requires genuine novelty
- More room for methodological detail than Nature
- Supplementary can be very extensive
- Emphasize reproducibility — reviewers check data/code availability statements carefully
- Strong figures with standalone captions are essential
