# Nature

## Overview
- **Full name**: Nature
- **Publisher**: Springer Nature
- **Scope**: Groundbreaking research across all fields of science and technology. Publishes papers of exceptional significance, originality, and broad interest.
- **Audience**: Broad — scientists across all disciplines, policymakers, science journalists
- **Impact Factor**: ~64 (2024)

## Article Types
| Type | Word Limit | Figures | Abstract |
|------|-----------|---------|----------|
| Article | ~3,000 words (main text) | 6 display items (figures + tables) | ~150 words, unstructured, no refs |
| Letter (legacy) | ~1,500 words | 4 display items | ~150 words |
| Brief Communication | ~1,000 words | 2 display items | ~100 words |
| Review | ~6,000 words | 8 display items | ~200 words |
| Perspective | ~3,000 words | 4 display items | ~150 words |

## Formatting
- **Sections**: No numbered sections. Introduction (untitled), Results, Discussion, Methods (at end or online-only). No separate Conclusions section.
- **References**: Numbered in order of appearance, superscript in text. Max ~50 for Articles.
- **Citation style**: Numbered, superscript (e.g., "...as shown previously^{1,2}.")
- **Equations**: Minimize; keep mathematical details in Methods or Supplementary.
- **SI units**: Required.

## LaTeX
- **Document class**: No official class. Use `article` with Nature-style formatting.
- **Bibliography style**: `naturemag.bst` or `nature.bst`
- **Template**: Nature provides a Word/LaTeX template on their website.

## Writing Style
- Accessible to a broad scientific audience — minimize jargon
- First paragraph: context and motivation (no heading)
- Final paragraph of main text: broader implications
- Active voice preferred
- Very concise — every sentence must earn its place
- Figures should be understandable without reading the main text

## What Reviewers Look For
- Exceptional novelty and significance
- Broad impact beyond one subfield
- Robust methodology with appropriate controls
- Clear, compelling narrative
- Strong visual presentation

## Tips for Acceptance
- The opening paragraph must grab a non-specialist reader
- The "Here we show..." statement must be extraordinary
- Methods rigor must be unquestionable (even if details go to Supplementary)
- Frame the work in the broadest possible context
- Supplementary Information can be extensive
