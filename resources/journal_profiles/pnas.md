# PNAS (Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences)

## Overview
- **Full name**: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America
- **Publisher**: National Academy of Sciences
- **Scope**: Original research of exceptional importance across biological, physical, and social sciences. Emphasizes broad scientific significance.
- **Audience**: Broad — multidisciplinary scientists
- **Impact Factor**: ~12 (2024)

## Article Types
| Type | Word Limit | Figures | Abstract |
|------|-----------|---------|----------|
| Research Article | 6 pages (~4,500 words) | 6 display items | 250 words, unstructured |
| Brief Report | 4 pages (~3,000 words) | 4 display items | 250 words |
| Perspective | 4 pages | 2 display items | 100 words |
| Review | 8 pages | 8 display items | 250 words |

## Formatting
- **Sections**: Significance Statement (120 words), Abstract, Introduction (untitled), Results, Discussion, Methods (can be brief in main text with SI Appendix for details).
- **Significance Statement**: REQUIRED. 120 words max. Plain language explaining why this work matters. Written for a broad audience. This is unique to PNAS and critical for acceptance.
- **References**: Numbered in order of appearance. Typically 40-60.
- **Citation style**: Numbered, inline (e.g., "(1, 2)" or "Smith et al. (1)")
- **Keywords**: Required (3-5)

## LaTeX
- **Document class**: `pnasresearcharticle` (PNAS provides their own class)
- **Bibliography style**: `pnas.bst` or `pnas2011.bst`
- **Template**: Official PNAS LaTeX template available on their website.

## Writing Style
- More formal than Nature but still accessible
- Significance Statement is written for a "scientifically literate but non-specialist reader"
- Results can include interpretation (Results and Discussion can be combined)
- Methods can be abbreviated in main text with full details in SI Appendix
- Quantitative and precise

## What Reviewers Look For
- Broad significance across disciplines
- Rigorous methodology
- Clear Significance Statement that justifies PNAS-level importance
- Well-organized, logical progression
- Appropriate use of SI Appendix

## Tips for Acceptance
- The Significance Statement is arguably the most important part — write it carefully
- Contributed articles (by NAS members) have different review, but direct submissions should emphasize broad impact
- PNAS values interdisciplinary connections
- SI Appendix can be extensive — use it to keep the main text focused
- Frame findings in terms of national/global importance
