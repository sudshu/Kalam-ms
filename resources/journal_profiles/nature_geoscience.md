# Nature Geoscience

## Overview
- **Full name**: Nature Geoscience
- **Abbreviation**: Nat. Geosci.
- **Publisher**: Springer Nature
- **ISSN**: 1752-0894 (print), 1752-0908 (online)
- **Frequency**: Monthly
- **Scope**: Multi-disciplinary journal covering the entire spectrum of Earth sciences — field work, modelling, and theoretical studies. Publishes significant, high-quality research that advances understanding of Earth, its environment, and the solar system.
- **Disciplines covered**: Atmospheric science, biogeochemistry, climate science, geobiology, geochemistry, geoinformatics and remote sensing, geology, geomagnetism and palaeomagnetism, geomorphology, geophysics, glaciology, hydrology and limnology, mineralogy and mineral physics, oceanography, palaeontology, palaeoclimatology and palaeoceanography, petrology, planetary science, seismology, space physics, tectonics, volcanology
- **Audience**: Geoscientists across all subdisciplines, climate scientists, atmospheric scientists, environmental scientists, and the broader scientific community
- **Impact Factor**: ~15.9-16.1 (2024-2025)
- **CiteScore**: 25.9-28.3
- **SJR**: 5.138 (Q1)
- **h-index**: 280
- **CiteScore Rank**: 2/198 in Earth and Planetary Sciences

## Formatting

### Manuscript Structure (Articles)
- **Opening paragraph**: Context and motivation (no heading — serves as introduction)
- **Results**: Main findings with subheadings permitted
- **Discussion**: Interpretation and broader implications (no subheadings)
- **Online Methods**: Up to 3,000 additional words; divided by topical subheadings; integrated into HTML and PDF; copy-edited; references fully indexed
- No separate "Introduction" or "Conclusions" heading
- No footnotes

### Abstract
- Up to 200 words, single paragraph
- Unreferenced (no citations)
- Should summarize the key finding and its significance

### References and Citations
- **Citation style**: Numbered in order of appearance, superscript in text (e.g., "...as shown previously^{1,2}." or "...across studies^{1-4}.")
- **Reference format**: Numbered list; titles of cited articles are required for all long-form manuscripts (Articles, Review Articles, Perspectives)
- **Guideline**: ~50 references for Articles
- **No footnotes or endnotes**: Only numbered references
- **Grant numbers**: Not permitted as numbered references; place in Acknowledgements

### Supplementary Information
- Submit as a single combined PDF file (text, figures, simple tables, legends)
- Complex tables or large datasets: separate Excel workbook ("Supplementary Tables" or "Supplementary Data")
- Each SI item must be cited at least once in the main text or Methods
- Use the word "Supplementary" each time an SI item is mentioned
- SI is not copy-edited (unlike Online Methods)

### Display Items
- Figures should be understandable without reading the main text
- Figure legends: descriptive title followed by panel-by-panel descriptions
- Extended Data figures/tables allowed as online-only display items

### General Formatting
- Preferred font: 12-point Times New Roman
- SI units required
- Equations: minimize in main text; keep mathematical details in Methods or Supplementary
- Acknowledgements: brief; do not thank anonymous referees or editors; grant numbers are acceptable

## LaTeX

- **Document class**: No official NGeo class. Use `article` with Nature-family styling, or standard classes (`revtex.cls`, `amsart.cls`).
- **Bibliography style**: `naturemag.bst` (same as Nature)
- **Springer Nature template**: Available on Overleaf (`sn-jnl` class with `sn-nature` bibliography style) — acceptable but not required
- **BibTeX handling**: Copy the reference list from `.bbl` file into the main `.tex` file; delete `\bibliography` and `\bibliographystyle` commands
- **Submission**: All text (including references, tables, figure captions, Online Methods) in a single `.tex` file
- **Initial submission**: PDF, Word, or TeX/LaTeX accepted; if using TeX/LaTeX, submit compiled PDF

## Data and Code Availability

### Data Availability
- **Mandatory** data availability statement required
- Since January 2019: data must be shared through community repositories where available
- Where community repositories do not exist: use general repositories (Figshare, Dryad, Zenodo)
- Data must be accessible to support the conclusions of the paper
- Accession codes, unique identifiers, or web links must be provided

### Code Availability
- Custom code central to the main claims must be made available for peer review
- Code should be accessible to readers upon publication
- Recommended: deposit in a persistent repository (Zenodo, GitHub with DOI)
- Include version information and dependencies

## Editorial Process

### Selection Criteria
Editors evaluate manuscripts based on:
1. **Advance in understanding**: Does it significantly advance the field?
2. **Soundness of conclusions**: Are the claims well-supported by evidence?
3. **Broad relevance**: Will it interest the journal's diverse readership, not just specialists?
4. **Data quality**: Technically sound, obtained with appropriate techniques, carefully analyzed and interpreted?

### Desk Rejection
- **~80-85% of submissions are desk-rejected** without external review
- Only ~15-20% of initial submissions are sent for peer review
- Desk rejection decisions typically take 2-33 days (median ~1-2 weeks)
- Editors screen for significance, broad relevance, and technical soundness
- Rejected papers may be offered transfer to other Nature Portfolio journals

### Peer Review
- Papers sent for review: ~50% are ultimately accepted after revision
- **Overall acceptance rate**: Estimated ~8-10% of all submissions
- Typical review timeline: ~3 months for peer review phase
- Usually 2-3 reviewers per manuscript
- Full-time professional editors manage the process (not academic editors)

### Transfer System
- Desk-rejected papers may be transferred to other Springer Nature journals
- Authors can opt in or out of the transfer cascade
- Common transfer destinations: Nature Communications, Communications Earth & Environment

## What Editors and Reviewers Look For

1. **Significance beyond the subfield**: Results must matter to geoscientists broadly, not just specialists
2. **Strong "Here we show" statement**: The central finding must be clearly stated and compelling
3. **Robust methodology**: Appropriate techniques, controls, uncertainty quantification, and sensitivity tests
4. **Compelling visual presentation**: Figures are the backbone of the paper; they must tell the story independently
5. **Accessibility to non-specialists**: Papers are substantially edited for accessibility; write for a broad geoscience audience
6. **Quantitative results**: Include numbers, error bars, confidence intervals — avoid vague qualitative claims
7. **Cover letter**: Must explain why the work is appropriate for NGeo's diverse readership; this is critical for surviving desk screening

## Tips for Acceptance

1. **Frame broadly**: The opening paragraph must convey why any geoscientist should care, not just your subfield
2. **Cover letter is critical**: Explain the broad significance explicitly — editors use this to triage
3. **Figures first**: Design your 4-6 main figures to tell the complete story before writing text
4. **Keep it concise**: 2,200 words is tight; every sentence must earn its place. Use Methods and SI for details
5. **Quantify everything**: Replace "significant increase" with "23% increase (p < 0.01)"
6. **Preempt reviewer concerns**: Address limitations proactively in Discussion; include sensitivity analyses in SI
7. **Data and code ready**: Have your data repository and code archive prepared before submission
8. **Highlight novelty early**: The first paragraph should make clear what is new and why it matters
9. **Cross-disciplinary connections**: Draw explicit links to other geoscience subdisciplines
10. **Polish the abstract**: 200 words is your elevator pitch; it must stand alone and compel interest

## Recent Carbon Cycle / Satellite CO2 Papers in Nature Geoscience (2023-2025)

Nature Geoscience regularly publishes high-impact papers on carbon cycle dynamics, atmospheric CO2, and satellite observations. Recent themes include:

- **CO2 growth rate anomalies**: Papers investigating record atmospheric CO2 growth rates during the 2023/24 El Nino event and the role of reduced land carbon sinks
- **Satellite-based carbon monitoring**: Studies using OCO-2 and other satellite platforms for tracking global carbon flux variations
- **Terrestrial carbon cycle responses**: How climate variability (ENSO, droughts, heatwaves) affects land carbon sinks
- **Ocean carbon cycle**: Geological carbon cycle sinks in ocean crust, marine carbon sequestration
- **Carbon budget analyses**: Global and regional carbon budgets integrating satellite, in situ, and model data

The journal has strong interest in papers that:
- Use satellite observations to reveal new carbon cycle dynamics at global scales
- Quantify carbon sink/source changes during climate extremes
- Bridge atmospheric observations with surface flux estimates
- Provide early warning signals for carbon cycle disruptions
