# Nature Climate Change

## Overview
- **Full name**: Nature Climate Change
- **Publisher**: Springer Nature
- **Scope**: Research and analysis advancing understanding of climate change, its impacts, and responses, written for a broad interdisciplinary audience
- **Audience**: Climate scientists, Earth system scientists, policy-relevant researchers, and non-specialist scientists across disciplines

## Article Types
| Type | Main text | Display items | Abstract |
|------|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Article | Up to 3,000 words | Up to 6 figures/tables | Up to 150 words, unreferenced |
| Analysis | Up to 3,000 words | Up to 6 figures/tables | Up to 150 words, unreferenced |
| Brief Communication | 1,000-1,500 words | Up to 2 figures/tables | 3 sentences, up to 70 words |

## Formatting
- **Structure for Articles/Analyses**: Introduction (no heading), Results, Discussion, Online Methods
- **Subheadings**: Allowed in Results and Online Methods; Discussion should not use subheadings
- **References**: Numbered in order of appearance; guideline of about 50 for Articles and Analyses
- **Supplementary files**: Supplementary Information is allowed and commonly used for specialist detail
- **Extended Data**: Optional online-only display items can be included when essential background should not count against the main display-item budget
- **Submission package**: Manuscript file should include Methods and figures; the cover letter should explain broad relevance to the journal readership

## LaTeX
- **Document class**: No official class; use `article` with Nature-family styling
- **Bibliography style**: `naturemag.bst`
- **Template**: Nature Portfolio generic submission templates are acceptable

## Writing Style
- Broad climate relevance must be clear in the opening paragraphs
- Main text should stay concise and quantitative
- Figure legends must be standalone and intelligible without the main text
- Results should emphasize substantive climate-science advance, not only method development
