# Methods

## Model and sites

Simulations use a parsimonious terrestrial carbon model that tracks foliar, wood, root,
litter and soil organic carbon pools with prescribed allocation fractions and
temperature- and moisture-dependent turnover [@Author2018Dalec]. Four tropical forest
sites span a wet-to-seasonally-dry gradient. Combustion was disabled at all sites in
both members of every pair, so no part of the reported difference is attributable to
fire.

## Parameter ensembles

Each site carries 200 parameter sets retained after constraining the model against
site-level carbon stocks and fluxes, giving 800 site-parameter simulations. Both members
of a pair use the same parameter set and the same equilibrated initial pools, so within
a pair the only difference is the climate forcing during identified extreme months.

## Extreme identification and counterfactual splicing

A month is classified as a joint hot-dry extreme when it exceeds the 90th-percentile
monthly-mean temperature threshold, exceeds the 90th-percentile vapour-pressure-deficit
threshold, and falls below the 10th-percentile precipitation threshold, all computed
per site and per calendar month over the full record.

The repeated-extremes run uses the observed seasonal cycle, the long-term climate
trends, and all anomalies. The no-extremes counterfactual is identical except that in
identified extreme months the temperature, precipitation and vapour-pressure-deficit
anomalies are replaced by non-extreme values drawn from the same calendar month. The
seasonal cycle and the trends are untouched in both runs, which is what makes the
year-20 stock difference attributable to the extreme anomalies rather than to a
difference in mean climate.

## Carbon debt

The carbon debt is

*D*~20~ = *C*~no-extremes~(20 yr) − *C*~repeated-extremes~(20 yr),

evaluated on total ecosystem carbon. A positive *D*~20~ means the run that experienced
the extremes ended with less carbon. Site values are reported as the median over the
200 parameter sets; pooled values as the median over all 800 simulations with a 95%
interval taken as the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the same distribution.

## Flux recovery

Recovery is diagnosed per event as the number of months until monthly net biome
production re-enters the site's baseline interquartile range, computed from non-extreme
months at that site, and remains inside it. Events whose recovery window overlaps a
subsequent extreme are excluded from the recovery statistics but retained in the stock
calculation.

## Placebo test

The neutral-splice placebo repeats the full procedure while replacing the same number of
randomly selected non-extreme months at each site. A debt that arises from the splicing
operation rather than from the extremes would appear here at comparable magnitude.

## Process decomposition

The pooled median debt is decomposed factorially into the contribution from lower
cumulative net primary production, the contribution from additional ecosystem
respiration, and a residual interaction term arising from the dependence of pool
turnover on pool size. Shares are reported as percentages of the pooled median debt. The
decomposition is internal to the model and does not establish the dominant mechanism in
real forests.

## Robustness

Three sensitivities were run: the extreme-definition percentile (85th, 90th, 95th),
the numerical-optimizer seed, and monthly rather than daily aggregation of the climate
forcing. A fourth test narrowed the retained parameter bounds. Results are in
Supplementary Fig. S1, Supplementary Table S1 and Supplementary Table S2.

## Data availability

The synthetic forcing and outputs are generated by `figures/make_demo_figures.py`. No
observational dataset underlies this demonstration.

## Supplementary Note S1 — Why the wettest site carries the largest debt

The ordering of site medians runs opposite to the intuition that drier forests are more
extreme-sensitive. Two features of the design account for it. First, extremes are
defined per site by percentile, so every site experiences an equally rare event rather
than an equally severe one in absolute terms. Second, the wet site sustains the highest
baseline productivity and the largest carbon pools, so the same fractional suppression
removes more absolute carbon. The seasonally dry sites are already water-limited for
part of each year, and an additional hot-dry month there displaces proportionally less
uptake. The sign of this ordering is a property of a percentile-based definition and
would likely change under an absolute-severity definition.
